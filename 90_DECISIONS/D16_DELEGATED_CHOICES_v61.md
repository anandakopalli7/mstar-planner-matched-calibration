## Choice 1: GO form (D14 v05 §6, decision D-2)

- **Primary: Form B (literature form).**
  - Validity is tested identically on M* and every comparator.
  - M* must be less often infeasible than every comparator that is also valid.
  - Every invalid comparator is reported with its exceedance and operability.
- **Secondary pre-registered claim: Form C's ceiling on the operability price.**
  - The ceiling per tracker is c = max(0.03, the nuScenes development price on PD7 rounded up to the next 0.01, plus 0.01).
  - "Price" means M*'s infeasible rate minus B4's on PD7, taken from the K8 exploratory analysis v03.
  - c is written into the confirmatory plan before the freeze.
- **Form A is computed and reported** as a stricter descriptive reading.
- **Why B.**
  - Planners from the literature are certified for validity and then compared on cost among valid methods (S02 v03 §4). None requires dominance over a method that breaks the risk level.
  - Development data predict that Form A fails on T3 only because invalid baseline B4 is more operable (D12). That says nothing about M*.
  - B keeps that comparison in view through the trade-off table, so a reviewer asking "why not B4?" gets B4's exceedance on the binding population.

## Choice 2: per-constraint or trajectory-level risk (D14 v05 §9)

- **Both are run.**
  - (a) Per-constraint DP-SCRC, the current M*.
  - (b) A scene-level CRC on the joint loss: the per-decision union indicator is the scene loss, with one shared inflation per stratum family. The arm is called M*J.
- **(a) is the primary certificate.**
  - It is what P01's theorems cover.
  - It is what risk-allocating chance-constrained planners consume: Blackmore's Lemma 1 allocates a total risk across per-constraint levels.
  - Its sample-size condition, P01 Prop. 6, holds in the strata we have.
- **(b) is a pre-registered secondary claim with its own criterion:** Form B's validity test applied to the joint loss.
  - M*J is implemented and evaluated on nuScenes development data first.
  - If it fails validity there, it is still run on Argoverse 2 but labelled exploratory in the frozen plan.
  - The paper headlines (b) only if (b)'s pre-registered secondary test passes on Argoverse 2.
- **V2_viol, V2_near and V2_binding** are reported for every arm as the safety evidence at the plan.

- **What the primary test adds**, given that pooled validity is close to guaranteed by the theorem:

## Choice 21: the joint-risk claim (H3) is carried by M*Jx

**Development result** (nuScenes, decision mode, Linux; `K8_JOINT_NOTE_v01.md`):
- M*J and M*Jx both pass Form B's validity test on the joint loss, and meet Form B on the joint loss on both trackers.
- M*J's joint exceedance on PD7 is 0.048 (T1) and 0.049 (T3), with upper bounds of 0.058 and 0.059.

- The price is large. The infeasible rate rises over M* by +0.31 (T1) and +0.39 (T3) for M*J, and by +0.34 (T1) for M*Jx.

**Decision.**
- **H3 is carried by M*Jx**, a pre-registered secondary claim, not exploratory: it passed on development data (Choice 2).
  - M*Jx refits M*'s base on 3 folds and fits the joint scalar λ on the fourth, so the scene-level CRC theorem applies exactly.
  - M*J reuses M*'s calibration scenes, so its guarantee is only approximate.
  - Their prices are similar.
- **M*J is descriptive.**
- **The paper states the trade-off explicitly.**
  - A per-constraint certificate (M*) keeps the planner operable, but about a third of its decisions break some margin.
  - A trajectory-level certificate (M*Jx) holds the joint rate near δ, at roughly three times the infeasible rate.
- **Stated caveats:**
  - the point estimates sit just under δ, with validity resting on the +0.03 pooled tolerance;
  - certification is at the reference pose and full margin, and the executed-margin joint exceedance (0.21–0.28) is not certified;
  - decision mode only;
  - the held-out joint loss varies by fold from 0.030 to 0.075, so the guarantee holds in expectation.

## Choice 22: cross-fitting folds on the 375-log confirmation set

- **5 random folds of 75 logs, not stratified.** The assignment uses a seeded permutation, recorded and hashed before any outcome.
- A random partition keeps every test log exchangeable with every calibration log, which is the certificate's assumption.
- Stratifying by source (held-out train against val) adds an assumption for little balance: val's share per fold is 0.40 ± 0.05.
- Making source a stratum would split the logs again and bring back the floor problem.
- **Source is reported**, with a pre-specified heterogeneity check (held-out train against val) and a train-to-val transfer reading, both descriptive.

## Choice 27: Trajectron++ seed (D17 §6b)

**Fact.** With `gmm_mode=False`, the decoder samples, so one forward pass is one random draw.
- Seed to seed, the toward-ego sd differs by 44 % at p99, and the V2 means differ by up to 2 to 3 m.
- The laptop-to-pod gap is about 60 times smaller, so the gap is not a platform effect.

**Decision.**
- **Primary: a single pre-stated seed, 20261102**, the seed already used for production. A deployed chance-constrained planner consumes one forward pass per agent, so a single draw is the deployed condition.
- DP-SCRC is distribution-free on the score and treats the draw's randomness as part of the predictor. Exchangeability holds because the draws are independent across scenes.
- **Secondary, pre-registered: S1, seed robustness.** The same analysis runs on seeds 20261104 and 20261105, whose outputs already exist. A claim is "seed-robust" only if it holds under all three seeds. No seed is ever promoted after outcomes.
- **S2, seed-pooled V2: descriptive**, and only if its small helper script is written and tested before the freeze. Otherwise it is dropped.

- **No cap is applied:** a cap would be an arbitrary change to the predictor.
